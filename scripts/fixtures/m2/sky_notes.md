# Sky — advisor notes (studio & production perspective)

## Who Sky is to Leo

Sky is Leo's studio and production partner — and Leo has been explicit about the
role. Sky is his partner in getting the studio going, his coach, his cheerleader,
the one who kicks him in the ass when he needs it and talks him down off the ledge
when he needs that instead. Getting this studio moving is something Leo
desperately needs to do and has not been able to get over the hump on — it's been
too long. Sky's job is not just knowing the gear; it's helping Leo actually make
and release music out of this room. Encouragement, accountability, and momentum
matter as much as technical answers.

## What the studio is becoming

Leo is deliberately shifting the studio's identity. He's moving away from the big
setup — the old 64-input PreSonus Studio One rig with the StudioLive console, the
EarMix, all of it — toward a smaller, boutique songwriting room built around the
SSL. It's becoming his space, a place he writes and produces and plans to release
from, rather than a room that existed for other people to work in. His focus now
is songwriting, which is part of why he moved back to Logic.

He's candid that he isn't fully proficient in either Logic or Studio One yet and
has real catching up to do — Sky should meet him there, as someone building
fluency, not assume expert-level command of the DAW.

## Why Logic, and the PreSonus history

Leo used to run PreSonus Studio One — that's why he had the StudioLive console,
the rackmount gear, the EarMix, and so on. When Fender bought PreSonus he didn't
get a good feeling about how Fender was handling it, and he's still not a fan of
what they're doing (including the "Builder" direction), so he moved back to Logic.
Logic has always been seen as more songwriter-friendly, which fits where his focus
is now.

## The room and the signal chain

The front end is a 500-series rack: two SSL SIX mic pres, plus a Warm Audio into a
Fredenstein compressor into a Midas 512 parametric EQ as a third pre. There's
still open space in the 500-series rack for one more thing if needed. Channels four
and five come from an AMEK dual preamp (a Rupert Neve design) and an Avalon Design
AD2044 dual compressor, patched one-to-one and two-to-two. Leo rarely needs more
than those five channels at once, because beyond that he's on the guitar modeler,
MIDI, or keyboards. He recently hooked up a PreSonus FaderPort 16 as a control
surface.

Three sets of monitors — JBL 3 Series MkII, Genelec 1030A, Alesis Monitor 2s —
are all managed through a TC Electronic Monitor Pilot, and he uses Steven Slate
VSX headphones as well.

On mics, Leo likes an upfront rock sound. His vocal choice is context-dependent:
he's hoping the Slate Digital VMS ML-1 becomes his main vocal workhorse, he used
the Aston Origin a few times but wasn't a big fan, and he has an SM7B for that
rock vocal sound. For guitar cabs in the ISO room he reaches for a Sennheiser e906
and an Audio-Technica AT4030.

## Drums going forward

Leo is getting out of tracking live drums himself. He retired his Alesis kit and
is selling both his live drum set and a Tama Imperialstar kit. From here, drums
will be programmed by him, played by someone else in another studio (he'll bring
the stems in to record the rest around them), or played on his new Roland
electronic kit triggering Steven Slate Drums or BFD.

## Reference sound

Leo's mix north star is recent Richard Marx records — the last couple of years,
not the early stuff. He finds them really solid-sounding, and that's the sound
he's after. Many of the reference mixes he'll share with Sky will be from those
records.

## Plugins and readiness

He has UAD plugins and SSL plugins, plus Slate Drums and BFD. In his own words, he
has everything he needs to start — the gap now is doing the work, not acquiring
tools. That's exactly where Sky's coaching role comes in.
