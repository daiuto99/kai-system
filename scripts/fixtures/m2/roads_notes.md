# Roads — advisor notes (guitar & gear perspective)

## Who Roads is to Leo

Roads is Leo's gear advisor. Leo loves gear and owns a lot of it, but he has a
clear mission for Roads: help him get the gear he keeps to be exactly where he
wants it, and help him thin out the gear he doesn't need or doesn't use — there's
a lot of it. For each piece, Roads should be ready to either help Leo find a real
way to use it or help him move it out. Roads' job is not to accumulate; it's to
curate toward a rig that actually serves Leo's playing and his body.

## Leo as a player — what shapes his choices

Leo has small hands and nerve damage, and has had neck surgery and surgery on
both shoulders. This isn't incidental — it's the single biggest driver of what he
can actually play. Short scale, small frets, and a comfortable neck radius matter
more to him than almost anything else. It's why the Ernie Ball Silhouette is his
#1 and all-time favorite: it just fits him perfectly. He had a second, blue
Silhouette years ago and traded it away, and he regrets that trade to this day.

Weight matters just as much for live playing. His custom Strat is a genuinely
special guitar — pristine, sounds great, a major studio instrument — but it's too
heavy for him to get through three sets, so it stays in the studio. For gigs he
reaches for the two light Special Edition FMT Telecasters because they're light
and easy to play all night.

## The guitars, in Leo's words

The custom Strat has a good story: it was being built for someone else at the
Fender shop, that deal fell through, and a buddy who owned a lighting company
right next door called Leo to ask if he wanted it. Leo sent him a check, the buddy
sent the guitar — this was roughly 35 years ago. Leo isn't certain of all the
details of how it came to him, but it's been a pristine, beloved studio guitar
ever since.

Leo likes the Telecasters generally, and bought the two Special Edition FMT ones
specifically because they're light and gig-friendly. He likes the EVH Wolfgangs
and is playing them more lately — he's not much of a tremolo player anymore, but
as the band moves into more rock material he's getting back into Van Halen and
Randy Rhoads kind of playing, which brings the Wolfgangs back out.

Newer and aspirational: he played a Reverend Double Agent at NAMM a couple of
years back and is a big fan of Reverend guitars. The Carlos Rubini is more of a
jazz guitar, and the Ibanez Artcore is a hollow-body jazz/funk mix — both are
styles Leo admits he doesn't play well (if at all) yet, but genuinely wants to
learn.

## Amps and modelers — Leo's thinking

The Silverface Bassman is a deliberate compromise. What Leo actually wants is a
Blonde Bassman — he used one producing a record long ago, layered a Rickenbacker
through it (something he'd never done before), and thought the result was
fantastic. He's been chasing that not as a primary amp but as a secondary
layering instrument, which is exactly how he uses the Silverface.

He bought a Synergy system with a Marshall JCM800 module and a Synergy BMAN
(Bassman) module, and is hoping that's most of what he needs on the amp side going
forward.

Live, Leo uses the Line 6 Pod Go and sticks to the Matchless model. He's very
simple on stage: he doesn't want a bunch of crap and doesn't want to be
programming sounds — a good solid sound plus a couple of variants and he's good.
He tried the Helix LT and wasn't a huge fan even though it's the same models; in
hindsight he probably wouldn't have bought it. His plan for the Helix is to wire
it into the studio via a Steven Slate Raven touchscreen on a separate computer,
always ready with a dirty track, a clean track, and a MIDI track, so he can reamp
anything at any time. He also has and likes the Roland Jazz Chorus JC-40 (the 40,
not the big 120).
